# Laser workspace (L…)

The laser cutter built into SpindleLabs (Ben, 2026-11-11: "a built in laser cutter"). Behind `?laser` until Ben releases it. No kernel files: shapes come from Design through the representation registry, 2D curve maths stays in the kernel (offsets for kerf come with K1, step 52).

The goal (Ben, 2026-11-12): Laser is the laser program itself. A person goes from the drawing to the running machine here, with no other program in between: layers with speed and power, a preview with the job's time, a frame run, and the job sent to the laser from the browser. The files (SVG, DXF) stay as the way out to any machine Laser can't talk to yet, and are never dropped.

Ben has no laser of his own; he uses his school's ("a big red one", make and controller not known yet; most likely a CO2 machine with the kind of controller school lasers have, which L5 is for). So the open, documented controllers (GRBL) are built first, checked against a simulator and never against a guess, and the school kind is researched alongside (L5a can start any time: it decides whether Ben can use this at school at all).

Rules for everything that reaches a machine (L4, L5; the shared ones are in `docs/integration-map.md` row 25, roadmap I15 and `docs/ai.md`):
- Nothing moves or fires by itself. A job starts only when a person presses Start on the confirmation screen (`HumanConfirmation`, `packages/core/src/model/machines.ts`); the assistant can prepare a job and never send one.
- Stop is on screen the whole time a machine is connected, and the machine's own interlocks (lid, water, emergency stop) are never bypassed or worked round.
- Before the beam: the person is asked to be at the machine, and a job that leaves the bed, or has a part off the bed, is refused in words.
- A lost connection is said at once, with what the machine does on its own in that case.
- The connection goes through the shared machine library and connection layer (I15), never a private one of Laser's.
- Controllers are learnt from their written documentation and from projects under permissive licences only (root CLAUDE.md, "When stuck"); a copyleft firmware or sender may be run as a separate checker, never read.

## Steps

- [x] L1a. The Laser page: Design's sketches and profiles listed (Design's `design:outlines` lister), laid out in rows on the bed (size, gap, sheet thickness kept in the project), drawn from above with the kernel's own extrude, saved as SVG and DXF at true size with exact arcs. Verified: hand-worked unit tests (layout, moves, the SVG and DXF text, bulge decimals), deliberate breaks each caught (bulge sign, open polyline, right-edge gap, spacing, a stale view drawn, bulge rounding, a suppressed sketch listed), the browser check `apps/fission/e2e/laser-page.mjs`, and an outside reader: ezdxf 1.4.5 reads the DXF as R12 in mm with no audit errors, and the areas from its own arcs match the hand-worked ones (slot 800 + 100π = 1114.159265359, quarter-arc triangle 2 + π/2 − 1 = 2.570796327, to 9 decimals); svgelements reads the SVG's paths closed at the right places.
- [x] L1a2. The flat view (Ben, 2026-11-12: "laser cutting is 2D"): the bed drawn straight from above as an outlined sheet at true scale with a ruler, each part as its outline with its name, sliding and zooming only (no orbit, no view cube, no Top/Front/Right; its own Zoom out, Zoom in and Fit in the bottom bar's look), over the shared viewport's empty 2D view so Design's camera comes back untouched. Drawn from the saved file's own path data, so the kernel is asked nothing and the screen can't differ from the file. The sheet-thickness box is gone until something uses it (the value a project holds is kept). Verified: hand-worked unit tests of the camera (fit, zoom about a point, limits, the ruler's steps, margins clear of the parts list; 0.9× and 1.1× at each limit), the view in happy-dom (what is drawn where, wheel, drag, pinch, keys), 22 deliberate breaks each caught, and the browser check at 1280 and 375 px (one true scale for bed and part, nothing 3D left, the wheel about the pointer, Design's camera the same after). A second reading of the whole change found seven edge bugs, each fixed with a test that failed first: no made-up bed while Design's shapes are being read, a drag whose release went missing no longer sticks, a phone sheet opening or closing over a fitted bed fits it again, no bed over "Coming soon" in the second pane, a move that moves nothing keeps a fitted view fitted, the phone's margins go by the window's width, a new gap leaves the view where it was; names out of sight are not drawn. Left for later (small): the view's zoom is lost when Undo or Redo rebuilds the page; the left ruler numbers can be cut off when the bed's left side is out of sight; the view's keys are not in the shortcut list; the Properties sheet on a phone (over 40% of the view) is not fitted round though the bed would fit above it.
- [x] L1b1. Parts placed by hand: a part dragged on the bed (a mouse drags the part it presses; a finger the picked one), moved by the arrows, turned by quarter turns (Turn in the row of buttons, R) or to a typed angle, and put at a typed X and Y, its place kept in the project by its id in Design (`LaserData.placed`). The first part moved fixes every part where it is so nothing else shifts; parts drawn later find room in rows round them; "Back in the rows" frees one and "Arrange in rows" all. A part past the bed's edge is said in words and marked on the bed (dashed, warning colour), never moved back, and the files still save with it where it was put. Verified: hand-worked unit tests of the turns, places and rows (place.ts), the view (picking, dragging to whole mm, fingers, keys) and the page (typed numbers, turns, refusals, rows) with 30 deliberate breaks each caught, and the browser check at 1280 (mouse) and 375 (finger). With `placed` Laser's data became version 2, and the version is now read (lost-work hunt, 2026-11-12: it was written and never looked at, so a build from before a new field would have stored the data again without it): version 1 opens as 2, a later version is refused as newer so the project is kept whole for a build that reads it, and a change in the page keeps any field it has no name for. Tests first, 10 deliberate breaks each caught.
- [x] L1b2. Overlap warnings, exact: each pair of parts whose boxes overlap or all but touch (one of them placed by hand) is asked of the kernel (`overlap.ts` `partsRelation`: apart, touch, overlap, or unknown with why), the answer kept for the two as they sit. An overlap is said in words and marked on both parts; a touch is said (their cuts would share a line) and not marked; too close to tell is said and marked; a check that fails says why. The files still save. The kernel's 2D answers do not cover every case yet (edges lying along each other, contact at a point), so the module goes a longer way round and says "unknown" where it can't tell; what the kernel would need to answer the question itself is filed with the kernel. Verified: hand-worked pairs on the real kernel (inside, sharing an edge, half a mm in, half a mm off), boundaries at the kernel's own lines, seeded pairs against number models, 22 + 20 deliberate breaks each caught, and the browser check with two sketches at 1280.
- [ ] L1b3. The picked part is the shared selection (a laser part is a Design sketch or profile, so picking it here picks it there), and the page's buttons for it come from the action registry. Small leftovers from L1b1: the picked part's size and place shown beside it on the bed; a part's row in the parts list picks it.
- [ ] L1c. Layers: cut, score and engrave, chosen per part in its row of the parts list (cut unless said; kept by the part's id as `LaserData.ops`). In the SVG a colour each, as laser programs map colours to layers (cut a red hairline, score a blue hairline, engrave filled black with no stroke), grouped engrave first, then score, then cut, so nothing is cut loose before it is engraved; in the DXF a layer each (CUT, SCORE, ENGRAVE, with the matching colour numbers; R12 has no fills, so an engraved part is its outline on ENGRAVE, said in the note). On the bed each is drawn in its own look, with a short key. Overlap warnings (L1b2) then apply to two cut parts only: an engraving or a score lying on a cut part is what they are for. Speed, power and passes come with L2a (the SVG and DXF files can't carry them). Starts from: `sheet.ts` `sheetSvg`, `dxf.ts` `sheetDxf` and `CUT_LAYER`, `page.ts` (the list rows in `draw`, `flatScene`, the pairs loop), `flat-view.ts` `drawScene` (a class per part).
- [ ] L1d. Kerf: the beam's width kept with the bed (a box beside the gap), and a warning where two cut parts are closer than it (the least distance between two parts is the kernel's to give: asked for with the overlap call, see L1b2; until it can, only what the boxes prove is said); then each cut moved out by half of it (holes in) from the kernel's 2D offsets (K1 with step 52), refused in words where a gap is narrower than the kerf.
- [ ] L1e. Flat faces of bodies as parts (a Design producer for a planar face's outline).
- [ ] L2a. Speed, power and passes for each layer (cut, score, engrave; an engraving also its line spacing), kept in the project, with the units a laser's own panel shows (mm/s, %). Refused in words outside the machine profile's limits.
- [ ] L2b. The machine profile, in the shared machine library (I15; DRAFT interfaces in core today): bed size (the bed boxes of L1a move there), which corner is the origin and which way y runs, the controller's kind, top speed, what number is full power, whether it homes. One made-up 400 × 400 mm profile until a real machine is known.
- [ ] L2c. Starting settings by material and thickness, in the shared materials library (I3, integration map row 29), each marked a starting point to be tried, never a promise; and a test card: a grid of small squares stepping speed one way and power the other, each row and column labelled by a scored number, so a person finds their own settings on their own sheet with no measuring tools.
- [ ] L3a. The beam's path in order (engrave, then score, then cut; inner loops before outer; nearest next), played as a preview on the bed with the travel moves shown faint, and the job's time from the speeds, the travel and the machine's acceleration, labelled an estimate. Hand-worked times for a square and a circle.
- [ ] L3b. The job as G-code for GRBL lasers, saved as a file (laser mode, dynamic power, the power number from the layer's % and the profile's full power; arcs as arcs where exact, else the path the kernel gives), behind the machine profile. Read back by an independent reader: every move inside the bed, the beam off on every travel, the path equal to the drawing.
- [ ] L3c. Engraving as fill: the scan lines across an engraved shape, each line's ends where it meets the outline (the kernel's to give: 2D curve maths; filed with the kernel before this starts), back and forth with overscan.
- [ ] L4a. Connecting to a GRBL laser over Web Serial (in browsers that have it; said in words where not, with the file of L3b as the way round): the machine's state, position and alarms read and shown, Home, moving the head by hand in steps, setting the sheet's origin. Nothing with the beam. Tested against a made-up serial port that speaks the documented replies, and a real controller's simulator run as a separate checker.
- [ ] L4b. A frame run: the head traces the job's box (or its outline) with the beam off, at a speed the person picks, so they see where the job lands on the sheet before anything is cut.
- [ ] L4c. Sending the job: the confirmation screen (what will run, on which machine, the time, the settings by layer, a short checklist), Start pressed by a person, the lines sent a few at a time so a Stop or a lost connection halts the head within a moment, Pause and Resume, progress and time left, the screen kept awake, a warning before the tab is closed, and the job written to the shared job history when it ends or is stopped.
- [ ] L5a. Research, no code: the controllers school and club CO2 lasers have. Known of Ben's school machine (2026-11-12): the job goes to the cutter as a file sent from the laser program (he doesn't yet know whether over a cable, the network or a memory stick, nor the file's kind; brand and model to come). What a browser can reach (USB as a serial port, the network, or only a job file carried on a memory stick), what is documented in writing, what permissively licensed projects show about the job format, and which route is safe. Ends with the route written here and L5b and L5c rewritten to match. Can run beside any step above.
- [ ] L5b. The job as that controller's own file (saved, then carried to the machine), with L2a's settings by layer inside it, read back by an independent reader.
- [ ] L5c. Sending to that controller directly, if L5a found a route a browser can take safely; with L4b's frame run and L4c's screen.

Order (11-12): L1b3, L1c, L1d, then L2a, L2b, L3a, L3b, L4a, L4b, L4c, with L2c, L3c and L1e fitted in where a thread is free; L5a as soon as a thread can take it.

Later ideas, not planned yet: nesting parts to save sheet, finger-jointed boxes from a solid (X13), engraving pictures from Image, sheet-metal flat patterns (step 87).
