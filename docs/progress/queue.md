# Job queue (Q…)

A side feature (Ben, 2026-11-13: "add the print and laser queue as a side feature of SpindleLabs and then continue with what we are doing"): a queue for a school makerspace. A student sends a 3D print or laser job, the file is checked automatically, the teacher approves it or sends it back with a reason, and approved jobs wait in a queue per machine, with material and machine time kept. It never slows the kernel roadmap or curved booleans: app files only, no kernel files, inside the 3-pull-requests-per-area rule. Later it may be sold as a school licence (about A$500 a school a year, `docs/plans/business.md` in the kernel repo); nothing here takes money.

How it is sold (Ben, 2026-11-13): one app and one codebase, never a separate product. "SpindleLabs for Schools" is a plan, priced per school per year (about A$500), with classes, the teacher's dashboard and Pro features for the school's students; the queue shows as a workspace only for school accounts, and a page for teachers comes later at spindlelabs.app/schools. The queue stays its own package (`packages/jobqueue`) and switch so it could be split out if it ever grows big.

How school accounts work (Ben, 2026-11-13): a school admin (usually the D&T head or IT) takes the Schools plan, which carries a number of student seats. The admin invites teachers by their school email, and only invited teachers can make classes. A teacher's class gets a short join code (and a QR code of it), with an optional cap on the class, all inside the school's seats. Students sign in with their school's Google or Microsoft account where the school allows it; for under-13s or schools without that, the teacher makes their logins (a name, the class and a picture password). At the end of the year the teacher archives a class, which frees its seats for the next one. The least-data rule above and the legal list (Q4: the school is the consenting party for its students, Ben's dad the holder) come before any of it reaches a real school.

First machines: Ben's school laser (VEVOR KH1490, 130 W CO2, 1400 × 900 mm bed, Ruida controller) and a Bambu P1S printer.

Rules for this workstream:
- Behind the `?queue` switch until Ben releases it. Nothing about it is public until the legal items (Q4) are checked.
- Minors use it, so personal data is the least that works: a display name the teacher types (for example "Sam K."), the class, and the jobs. No student email, photo, birthday or school ID is stored by the queue, none is shown to other students, and job files are deleted a set time after the job ends.
- It reuses what exists and never copies it: the print check, build area and slicer from `packages/print`, the laser layers and bed from the Laser workspace (`docs/progress/laser.md`), accounts from `packages/account`, and machine sending from the shared machine library (I15). Anything it needs from them is asked of that workstream, not built a second time.
- It never starts a machine by itself. A job is sent only when a person at the machine presses Start, with the same rules as Laser's L4 and Slice's H3.
- A check that cannot be done yet says so ("no time estimate yet"); it never shows a made-up number.
- Supabase SQL is pasted by Ben in the dashboard (his school laptop has no terminal); an applied migration is never edited; the server switch starts off.

## Steps

- [x] Q1. The queue's rules as plain code, `packages/jobqueue` (no page, no network, no personal data beyond the display name): a job (print or laser, file name and size, machine, material, the check's answers, estimated time and material), its states (sent, checked, waiting for the teacher, approved and queued, running, done, failed, sent back with a reason, withdrawn), which moves are allowed and by whom (student, teacher), the queue's order per machine (approval time, the teacher may move a job up or down), and the totals per machine and per student (time, material). Tests: hand-worked cases with the working shown, a refusal for every move not allowed, and seeded random sessions against a simple model. Done: `packages/jobqueue/src/queue.ts` (a failed run put back in the queue keeps its run, so its time and material still count); 11 tests including 200 seeded sessions against a table model; six planted breaks each turn a test red.
- [x] Q2. The automatic file check, `packages/jobqueue/src/check.ts`: a print job's STL (binary or text, `stl.ts`) goes through the P1S's build area (`buildArea`; sliced without the warm-up loop and brim when only they don't fit, said so), the print check (thin walls, sagging bridges, weak spots: notes for the teacher, not refusals), Slice's supports hint (sliced with supports when needed) and the slicing engine (`sliceStls`) for its time (whole minutes, up) and filament (grams to 0.1, PLA's, said for another material); a surface that isn't closed is refused in words before the engine is asked (the engine refuses one too). A laser job's SVG (`svg.ts`) or DXF (`dxf.ts`) is read for its outer box in millimetres (SVG: width/height against the viewBox, pixels at 96 to the inch or Illustrator's 72, said in a note; DXF: `$INSUNITS`) and its colours as Laser's layers (`LASER_COLOURS` and `LASER_DXF_COLOURS`, kept equal to Laser's `ops.ts` by a test): a red outline is cut, blue score, a black fill (DXF colour 7) engrave, any other colour refused with what to change. It is checked against the KH1490's 1400 × 900 mm bed (`KH1490_BED`, also turned a quarter turn) within `FIT_TOLERANCE` (1 µm, only for file decimals); the sheet used is the drawing's box; the time is null, shown as "Laser time: not yet", until L3a. Text, pictures, clones, hatches, dimensions and 3D entities are refused with what to do instead. `checkAction` makes the queue's check action (texts cut to 300 characters); the queue now keeps the check's notes on the job (`Job.notes`, up to 20). The boxes are exact (`box2d.ts`: lines, arcs and ellipses under any affine map at their four axis angles, Béziers at their derivative's roots); a DXF spline is sampled, with its control points' box as the most it can reach, and refused as "too close to be sure" when only that would cross the edge. Verified: 40 tests (hand-worked sizes with the working shown, every refusal in words, 0.9×/1.1× of the bed tolerance, 300 seeded curves under random maps against dense sampling, 200 seeded SVG and 200 DXF drawings against their corners worked apart, 24 seeded parts near the plate's limits on the real slicing engine against a model of the P1S's plate rules written from its description) and 29 planted breaks, each turning a test red. The box maths is TypeScript only because the kernel's 2D curve library has no Béziers yet (Vector V1): it moves there when it does.
