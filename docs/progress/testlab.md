# TestLab (the Simulate workspace)

TestLab is the Simulate workspace's name for people; its id stays `simulate`. The plan is T0–T7 in `docs/roadmap.md`. The solver and mesher are built in the private kernel; this file says only what the app does.

- [x] T0: the plan (roadmap PR #602).
- [x] T1: the live link, no solver, behind `?testlab`: a study on a Design body, held and pushed faces kept by stable name and found again after every design edit, a lost face said in words, out of date with a change note after an edit, Run checking the set-up and saying solving comes later. Tests: workspaces/simulate (unit, happy-dom), packages/testing `journeys/testlab-link.journey.ts` (real kernel), `apps/fission/e2e/testlab-page.mjs` (browser, 1280 and 375 px). Branch `claude/project-thread-mbndh6`.
- [x] T2: materials: core's one materials library made real for its mechanical half (`packages/core/src/model/material.ts`): PLA, PETG, ABS, aluminium 6061-T6 and A36 steel, each number with the document it came from (printed plastics from makers' data sheets, ISO 527/178 test bars at 100% infill, both along and across the layers, the stiffness the lower of the tensile and bending modulus; Poisson's ratios from two published measurements, PETG's assumed and said so). A study's material (TestLab data version 2; a version 1 study reads as "not chosen"), asked once in Made of with the numbers TestLab will use (the weaker way, across the layers, until the build direction is known) and every source. Bodies have no material of their own yet, so nothing is set from the body until they do (step 66). Tests: core `test/material.test.ts` (each number from its sheet by hand, every rule broken, Poisson's ratio at 0.9×/1.1× of 0.5), workspaces/simulate `test/materials.test.ts` and `test/study.test.ts` (version 1 read), `test/page.test.ts`; the journey and the browser check choose PLA.
- [ ] T3–T5: the solver, the mesher, and results in the shared view (kernel work first). The solver reads a study's material through core's `materialLibrary()` and `weakerWay()`; the strength used for the safety factor is the weaker way's, a tensile strength for printed plastics (said as "breaking strength"), a yield strength for metals.
